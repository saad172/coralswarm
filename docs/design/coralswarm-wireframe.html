how to read this screen
LAtom feed. Today's ingestion across all live connectors. Each row shows source, author, time. Your atoms and your colleagues' both appear — yours by direct ingestion, theirs via co-presence.
CReef graph. The extraction pipeline already linked today's atoms into a thread node ("migration") and surfaced a candidate decision. The dashed amber halo signals "needs your review."
RTeam reef. Promoted nodes — the slow-moving, durable shared graph. Activity below shows the live edits and ingestions you'd miss if you didn't open the app today.
design decisions
The node colors directly use the five swarm hues — coral, pink, amber, teal, plum — each tied to a kind. They appear on avatars, node borders, and badges so the user learns the system implicitly.
Headers use Instrument Serif italic for warmth ("the reef · today"). Mono labels carry machine info — counts, timestamps, source tags.
No notifications popover, no badges, no streaks. Calm by default — the design system's "posture: calm, organic, generous."
02 / ask the reef

Ask the reef.

Cmd-K anywhere opens this. The question feels like Instrument Serif italic — the warm voice of asking. The answer feels like Geist — the precise voice of the machine. Every claim cites the atom it came from; cards below let the user click through to source.

03 / candidates

Decision candidates.

The signature interaction. Extraction surfaces decision-shaped atoms; the user reviews, edits, and confirms — or rejects. Rejection trains future extraction. This queue is the only interaction the product asks of the user, and it's deliberately low-friction: at most a glance every couple of days.

how to read this screen
HThe framing matters. "Three things the reef thinks you decided" — not "review pending items." The product has a hypothesis; the user confirms or corrects.
CThe candidate card is a complete object. Title, rationale, alternatives, source atoms, co-presence. The user makes a decision (about the decision) without leaving the card.
⚖Confidence is shown, not hidden. 92% candidates look high-stakes. 41% candidates look tentative. The visual hierarchy maps to confidence — the third candidate is muted.
×Rejection is structured training. "Not a decision · train extractor" — a negative example loops back into the extraction prompt for future quality.
interaction notes
Each candidate borrows its left-border color from the dominant source: amber for time-sensitive work (queue), teal for system / customer thinking, ink-grey for low-confidence.
"Edit before confirming" opens a side panel — title, rationale, alternatives are all editable text. Extraction is a starting point, not an authority.

how to read this screen
H"The reef has been waiting for you." The product's voice spec applied to onboarding. Not "import meetings from your colleagues" — the reef has been doing the work, the user just arrived.
#Tally as proof of value. 12 meetings, 4 emails, 31 threads, 8 decisions — the user can see the magnitude of what's been documented around them before they make any choices.
⊠Pre-checked, not opt-in-per-item. Sensible defaults are checked; user can uncheck what they don't want. Friction in the loop kills it (system design §02).
business mechanics
This is the conversion screen — the moment the value of paid tier becomes legible. Co-presence intake requires cloud sync, which lives in the paid tier. The trial gives them 7 days to experience it.
Below the fold, "skip · empty reef" is a deliberate dignity escape — the product doesn't trap. Users who don't want this can keep walking.
06 / offboarding

When someone leaves.

The killer feature, named explicitly in the system design. A departing colleague becomes a structured, queryable brief: their decisions, their threads, the entities in their network. The team can ask the reef directly — "what was Ari's take on X?" — and get a cited answer from the work she already did. Days, not months, to ramp up the successor.

how to read this screen
BThe brief is auto-composed. Plum, not coral — the design system's color for archive and depth. The system has done the work; the team is reading the report.
DDecisions sit next to threads. Two ways to inherit work: "what she decided" and "what she was running." Active threads are flagged for new ownership.
QThe query block is the magic. "What was Ari's take on X?" — the successor can ask the reef the same way they'd ask Ari. Same cited answer pattern as Ask The Reef, but scoped to her contributions.
business stakes
This is where the dollars are. One mid-career departure = $20-60K of lost institutional knowledge. Offboarding mode is the explicit ROI story for enterprise buyers.
"Team key rotates on her last day" sits quietly in mono — the security story is there but doesn't shout.
